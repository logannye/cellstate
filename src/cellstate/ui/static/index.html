Does the interval cover at the rate it claims?

S6 · ADR 0024 + 0025

The state is inferred from NT_A and the predictive distribution it implies is scored against NT_B — ADR 0023's estimand, counted as coverage rather than aggregated into a ratio. One value per library, bootstrapped over the 14 libraries. This does not vary with rank: the estimand is fixed at the fitted configuration, so there is no knob here that would imply otherwise.

…

The verdict is the conjunction over every level below, never the reference row alone. The predeclared pair is coherent on the whole interval — below it the error bound would admit a coverage the floor rejects, above it the bound would ask for coverage over 1. ADR 0024 read that as a single point and gated at the interval's loosest member. A one-level gate is clearable by a constant: multiplying every predictive sd by 1.11 clears 0.90 with a bound of 0.0368, better than the shipped 0.0548. One level tests scale; scale is free.

Where the failure lives. S2's ratio reads as “uniformly ~16% too narrow”. Trim the worst 2% of the 1,400 gene-library outcomes and the spread is exactly earned — so the bulk is better than the interval claims and a handful of genes are far outside it. The two readings imply opposite repairs.
